About us · Bonjour means good morning

Relationship first. Everything else follows.

Bonjour Bébé is a small nursery, run since 2013 by its founder, an early-years and special-needs educator. We created a place where every child has time, an attentive adult nearby and room to discover the world in their own way.

Our values

Relationship before curriculum

Feeling safe comes first, learning follows. A child who feels secure reaches for new challenges on their own.

Respect for pace and boundaries

No punishments or rewards, in the spirit of Nonviolent Communication. We name emotions and stay with children through them.

Curiosity through the senses

Inspired by Montessori and Reggio Emilia: natural materials, low shelves and time to try things alone. We read aloud a lot.

Partnership with parents

Settling in with a parent in the room, a daily chat at pick-up, and all documents to read before you decide.

What makes us different

Small group, close bonds

A small group, attentive care and a warm, close atmosphere help children build relationships based on trust, with their caregivers and with each other. We make sure you always know who spends the day with your child.

Experience since 2013

The nursery is run on site by its founder, a qualified early-years educator and special-needs educator.

Specialist support

A psychologist, a speech therapist and a physiotherapist support our team. We notice early what a child needs and guide parents on next steps.

Settling in, Berlin model

A parent stays with the child in the room, and the child, not the calendar, sets how long settling in takes.

Healthy meals, nothing processed

According to our caterer, Zdrowy Maluch, meals are cooked from scratch, with limited sugar and home-grown vegetables in season.

Two kinds of help with fees

A City of Wrocław subsidy on every place (400 zł a month) and the „Aktywnie w żłobku” benefit. We help you through the paperwork.

Living or working in the city centre? We are at Dąbrowskiego 32/1U, a few minutes from Wrocław Główny station.

A start at your own pace

Settling in with a parent, step by step.

We follow the Berlin settling-in model. We don’t promise a fixed number of days: each next step follows your child’s signals.

  1. 1

    Getting to know you

    We talk about your family’s routines, habits and your child’s needs.

  2. 2

    Parent in the room

    Your child explores the new place with you nearby, while a caregiver gently builds a bond.

  3. 3

    A first short goodbye

    When we see your child is ready, we try a short separation, watch how it goes and talk it through with you.

  4. 4

    A longer day

    We extend the time step by step. What matters is how your child feels.

Senses and making

Sensoplastyka®

Creative play with natural materials: buckwheat, beans, rice, pasta, fruit, vegetables and home-made doughs. Children pour, knead, mix and discover new textures, colours, smells and sounds, with no set pattern and no judging the result.

This kind of play develops the senses, fine motor skills and concentration, and brings lots of joy.

Music and movement

Music Care and Gordon concerts

Music is part of our everyday nursery life. In sessions children listen, sing, play simple instruments and move to the rhythm. We don’t drill songs or judge anyone; what matters is the joy of making music together.

Grajki Małej Zgrajki (“Little Band’s Tunes”) is an original programme created by Natalia Bury, who runs Music Care. Here music is above all play and a way of being close to each other. Children discover the world of sound, rhythm and movement: they play instruments, sing, listen and respond to music with their whole body. No one has to do anything; every child takes part in their own way and at their own pace. Through play, musical sensitivity, coordination and attention grow.

Gordon concerts are prepared for us by Wytwórnik Muzyczny from Wrocław. They are based on Edwin Gordon’s music learning theory. Musicians play and sing live, often without words, and children listen however they like: sitting, lying down, dancing or walking up to the instruments. Just as with learning to speak, they first soak up music and only later start making it themselves.

  • Once a monthGrajki Małej Zgrajki with Natalia Bury
  • Twice a yearGordon concerts with Wytwórnik Muzyczny

Animals and nature

Meeting animals

Meeting an animal is a big experience for little ones. Children learn how to approach an animal calmly and safely; they watch, touch and feed it. Along the way they naturally develop empathy, patience and respect for other living beings.

Świat Alpak (Alpaca World), run by Estera Juzwenko, visits us with its alpacas. Children learn the alpacas’ names, where they come from and how to care for them, and then can stroke them and feed them carrots. The alpacas’ gentle nature helps children calm down, and playing near the animals supports coordination, concentration and self-confidence.

ZooMania brings smaller, tame animals to the nursery, for example little wallabies, pygmy hedgehogs, squirrels or sugar gliders. Children see them up close, learn what they eat and how they live, and can gently stroke some of them. It’s a nature lesson where the animal is real, not just a picture.

Words and imagination

Puppet theatre, musical tales and English

Stories, puppet shows, music and playing with language build speech, imagination and careful listening. The little ones don’t just watch: they sing, copy sounds and join in the story themselves.

Pani Teatrzyk (Mrs Puppet Theatre), Klaudia Kąca-Jasic, visits every month with an old suitcase full of hand puppets. Princesses, knights and animals pop out of it, and sometimes a real curtained stage appears. The short, simple stories suit the youngest children: they answer the characters, repeat words and live the tale together with them.

Muzobajki by Mariola Dereń visit us two or three times a year. They are fairy tales told through music. The performers build a story from simple props, colourful sets and everyday objects children know, with music played live. Children tap out the rhythm, listen to how the instruments sound and live the story together with its characters.
